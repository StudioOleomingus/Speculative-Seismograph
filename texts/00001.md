In the Nation of Memory, citizenship is not guaranteed by birth, documents, or digital databases. Instead, it exists only through **memory**. Every citizen must constantly prove their belonging to the nation by remembering and reciting the knowledge that defines it.

At the start of every year, the state distributes two artifacts to every citizen: **The National Memory Passport** and **The Annual Citizen’s Book of Memory**.

**The National Memory Passport**

The Memory Passport replaces traditional identity documents. Instead of storing personal information, it records a citizen’s **memory verification history**. Throughout the year, officials conduct random memory checks in places like transport hubs, government offices, and borders. Citizens must answer questions from the **Annual Citizen’s Book of Memory**.

Each correct answer earns a stamp in the passport. If a citizen fails to recall the required information, they may temporarily lose access to services, travel, or voting until they relearn it.

The passport therefore becomes a **record of how well a citizen remembers the nation**.

**The Annual Citizen’s Book of Memory**

Every year the government distributes the **Citizen’s Book of Memory**, a guide that all citizens must study and memorize. The book contains the official knowledge required to maintain citizenship, including national history, political structures, cultural values, borders, and civic responsibilities.

Because the content changes every year, citizens must constantly relearn and update what they remember to stay aligned with the nation.

**Citizenship Through Recall**

In the Nation of Memory, citizenship is not permanent but something that must be continuously performed through acts of remembering. The Memory Passport records these performances, while the Citizen’s Book defines what knowledge counts as belonging.
