# **1000 Maybe**

**Untitled.Vansh.Muley**

In this future, most technical work is performed by advanced AI systems.

Machines can analyze, design, and make decisions more efficiently than humans. Because of this, employers begin searching for something AI cannot truly replicate: **authentic human experience**.

**To prove this, society introduces the Certified Childhood Portfolio**.

From an early age, children must document emotional moments from their family life : *arguments, forgiveness, boredom, care, and disappointment.* These experiences are written down, verified by parents and schools, and organized into a portfolio.

**When applying for universities or jobs, people submit this documentation as** **proof of being human**. Employers believe that people who have lived through real family situations develop empathy and emotional understanding unlike AI.

Over time, families begin shaping their lives to produce better portfolio entries. Parenting books teach how to create artificial but valuable emotional situations. *Some families even stage conflicts or moments of care so children can record stronger experiences.*

**Childhood slowly becomes something that must be** **carefully documented and optimized**.

Through this speculation, the reader is invited to notice how in the real world personal experiences are increasingly being **recorded, curated, and used as proof of value**. 

This speculation pushes this idea to an extreme, asking what happens when even something as personal as childhood stops being simply lived and instead becomes **evidence to prove our worth as humans**.
