







#Untitled.11001
*By Priyanka*

**Paying with money is a thing of the past, now you can pay with your body-parts** *\- always available and extremely convenient in a cash crunch\!* 

If you cannot afford rent, groceries, or electricity, you can lease parts of your body to companies that need physical labor. *Your arms might sort packages, your legs might walk warehouse aisles, and your hands might assemble products you have never seen.*

The work happens inside monitored corporate facilities called Labor Centers. People check in, sign a legal agreement, and lie down in small pods. A neural interface connects to the base of the neck and a wearable product is provided. Within minutes, the brain enters a passive state, something close to sleep.  While the conscious mind rests, the company’s AI temporarily takes control of specific muscle groups through the nervous system. The body stands, walks, lifts, and repeats movements for hours, guided by software that treats muscles like programmable hardware.

When the session ends, the body returns to the pod. The participant wakes up with minor aftereffects like soreness, disorientation or organ failure or just a subtle hint of time skipping forward. Their account is credited, and the generous payment might cover food, housing subscriptions, or transportation for the week.

Companies describe the system as efficient. Building millions of robots is expensive and slow. Human bodies, however, already exist everywhere. They repair themselves, adapt to environments, and run on simple energy. **Renting bodies is cheaper than building machines.**

*Students can lease their bodies overnight to pay tuition. Workers can rent out their legs for walking shifts while they rest in pods.* Officially, body leasing is only allowed between individuals and certified corporate AI systems. Renting your body directly to another person is illegal, although underground exchanges still exist to allow people to avail of such amenities of modernity without the interference of the state.

All of this of course grows as a natural extension of what was once called the gig economy, in the great companies of the heyday \- like Zomato and Uber. 

If we are already moving toward a world where we no longer own things, why should our bodies be any different. We subscribe to software, stream entertainment, lease vehicles, and rent homes. **Access is replacing ownership \- let others access your body then, if you are not using it.**

Perhaps it is finally time to stop outsourcing just the small decisions to algorithms, and instead commit fully to  this paradigm \- **why not stop using technology and start becoming the biological hardware for it?**
