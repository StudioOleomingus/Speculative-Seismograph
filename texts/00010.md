







#Untitled.00010

*Wait\! Are you human? Solve 8 \+ 13 \= before you continue. Why should you stop and prove that you are human every time you open a website?* 

A small box appears and asks you to select images of bicycles, buses, or traffic lights. Sometimes you type distorted letters or drag puzzle pieces into place. These small actions feel ordinary now. We perform them without thinking, just to enter a website, log in, or download a file.

These **CAPTCHAs** were designed to separate humans from machines. They were supposed to prove that the person behind the screen is real. But something strange has started happening. Artificial intelligence can now solve many of these tests as well. Sometimes it even solves them faster than humans.

If machines can pass the same tests that humans do, then the question begins to change. The problem is no longer about detecting machines. Instead, it raises a deeper and more uncomfortable question: **How do we actually prove that we are human anymore?** 

This project imagines a world where humanity is measured in a different way. Instead of simple tests or tasks, society measures a subtle and invisible quality called **Existential weight.** This weight is not physical. It comes from small human moments when we pause, hesitate, reflect, doubt, or take time to think before acting. **These moments create a sense of presence. They show that a person is thinking, feeling, and existing with awareness.**

The more of this weight a person carries, the more human they appear to society. Their presence is noticed. Their thoughts and actions feel meaningful. They are *recognized, valued*, and *seen* as important. *In this world, being human is something rare and precious.*

But those who act too quickly begin to lose this weight. Instant responses, constant productivity, and automatic behavior make them appear lighter.

Without pauses or hesitation, their presence slowly fades. Society begins to overlook them. Their ideas carry less importance. Their actions seem to have no impact.

To become “*less human*” in this system is not simply to be ignored. It is to become weightless to exist without influence, without recognition, almost like a ghost moving through the world. Life continues, but it feels hollow, as if nothing truly matters.

**Here CAPTCHA is a savior \- we do endless CAPTCHAS to reassure ourselves of our humanity and increase our existential weight.**

Through this speculation we ponder how systems of efficiency are already shaping the way we value human presence. It asks viewers to think about a quiet but unsettling question.

Not simply, **Are you human?**

But something deeper:

**How much of you still is?**
