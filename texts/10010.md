







#Untitled.10010
*By Aarya & Amritaa*

What is the **fetus but a transit passenger, a freeloader?**

Imagine a future where pregnancy is no longer a solitary act of bodily endurance or love. Where it is not burdened by such silly antiquated cultural dictums. 

Where a developing fetus moves through different bodies like a *traveler between stations*, carried briefly by one person, transferred to another, incubated in machines designed to replicate the womb. **Where gestation is no longer bound to a single uterus, but fragmented, shared, distributed across multiple bodies and technologies \- all designed to optimise and speed up the process.** This is not science fiction. This is the logical endpoint of technologies already here.

In this landscape of technological mediation, the fetus is an asset in transit, moving between biotech incubators and human "infrastructure". This system explicitly **financializes the womb**, converting gestational labor into a series of tracked contributions, risk scores, and compensation tiers \- opening up in the process an untapped source of GDP.

Reproductive progress tends to benefit those with the capital to outsource risk, while marginalized bodies continue to absorb the physical toll under the guise of economic opportunity

We live in a world where reproduction is already technologically mediated, already separated from single bodies, already shaped by money and power. 

Most gestational workers are poor women in developing nations. They carry babies for wealthy international families and then surrender them, often with permanent psychological consequences and zero follow-up care. This is not hypothetical. **This is now.** Distributed pregnancy would simply intensify what's already real: the separation of gestational labor from motherhood, the economic desperation that makes "*choice*" meaningless, the erasure of the people who do the work. 

Distributed pregnancy forces the question we've been avoiding: if we can fragment pregnancy across bodies and machines, what does that tell us about how we value the work of gestation? About whose bodies we trust? About **what we're willing to sacrifice for "progress"?** This speculation is a mirror. What it reflects is us, our present contradictions, our comfortable silence, our complicity in systems we claim to oppose. The future described here may be speculative. But the questions it raises are urgent and real.  **What is consent? Whose bodies matter? What counts as labor?**
