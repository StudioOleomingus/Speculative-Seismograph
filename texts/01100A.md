***A monkey arrives in the mail.***  
***The monkey is not alone, the monkey came with a table***  
***Or maybe***  
***The table came with the monkey?***

You ignore the monkey, the monkey skitters away to join the other monkeys in your spare bedroom.

***The table is assembled.***  
***It looks nice in your living*** 

***A monkey walks*** 

*The monkey speaks “*

"This table is built with Grandwood Teak, Grandwood Teak is only found in the Western Hills near Algoa, It is a deciduous tree. Alphons, Eemer and Agel worked on your table..."

Another monkey walks in  
This monkey begins to describe the details of your couch, but only about the skin of the couch. 

It tells you the date which it was stitched together and about the color of the veins of the hand that pressed the button which then moved the cog which then moved a pulley which moved a needle and then where the metal was mined that made the needle and so and so forth. 

Another monkey walks in.

The three monkeys’ voices fade out as does your mind, and you peer through the window , over to your neighbours house. Her monkeys are out in the yard. They seem to be rambling to each other. They appear to not have been fed for a week.

You are thinking about buying a tshirt  
You are thinking where you might keep the inevitable monkey that will come with this new tshirt.

Another monkey walks in . . .

What would it look like if an entire species is bred and given out freely as an accessory alongside any and all objects that you buy \- just because we can. What if new monkeys were the byproduct of bioengineering our way into a new cycle of production? 

Afterall we already know that some of our most delicate ideas cannot be produced without the aid of monkeys (who are often found secreted away in buildings of power) \- what if everything is monkey-enabled and monkeyfied.

What do we do with extra monkeys that we end up producing? Do we send them to space? Or will they end up being exported for their meat? What happens when we can inevitably produce as much of any other species as we do toothpaste?
