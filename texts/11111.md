**Untitled.Heeya.Madhura**

When the world is a race to prosperity, why should you waste your hours sleeping when you can simply **take this unproductive time and freeze it for future use?**

**The Frozen Hours Protocol links a worker's biological clock directly to their productivity record.** If you take leave, need to stop working, or disappear from the labor system, your time is simply frozen. 

While the rest of society continues aging and progressing, you can have the luxury of remaining suspended for weeks or months. Take as much time as you need it will be there waiting for you when you return\!

You return to the past of course \- all your frozen time still unspent. But worry not because you can endure extended 24-hour shifts until your timeline catches up. No need to sleep just swallow your 

frozen time in the form of our patented still **Frozen Time Cubes**, each a compressed unit of  eight hours that can be consumed in just a few seconds.


	

To rest afterall is the most unnatural thing in the world \- your productivity is no longer a private thing, it affects the very foundations of our glorious nation\! No longer the tyranny of equal time when we can measure, distribute, and control it. It belongs to those who keep working, as it rightly should. *After all, have you ever seen your supervisors rest? Have you ever seen your bosses not make money? They earn even in their sleep and now you can too.*

The frozen hours protocol emerged from a need to turn the fallow hours of a society's time into something productive. Your time does not belong to you, it belongs to that which makes you a productive member. Visionaries like Mr. Narayan Murthy already foresaw this change, when pushing for 12 and 17 and 20 hour work days. 

But why stop at 24 hours when you can reach into the past and leap into the future of your time being productive. 

With our worker laws changing in unexpected ways \- perhaps we are already on our way towards such distortions of human worth and labour.

The idea of freezing time exaggerates this condition to reveal a troubling possibility: what if the right to age, progress, and exist in sync with society depended entirely on continuous labor?

**The speculation invites reflection on how systems already discipline bodies through time and how unequal access to rest reinforces broader social inequality.**
