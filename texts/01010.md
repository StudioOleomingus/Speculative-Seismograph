# **Untitled\_sanska Mayber**

**Untitled.Sanskar.Pattewar**

**In this speculative future, electricity is no**

**longer continuously available everywhere.**

Instead, it exists as a limited global pool that

circulates across regions through automated

infrastructure. A complex algorithmic system

distributes electricity based on demand,

environmental conditions, and priority

protocols. Rather than receiving constant

power, cities and communities experience

scheduled **“electricity windows”** when energy

becomes temporarily available.

Over time these distribution protocols

become extremely complicated and deeply

embedded in global systems, making them

difficult for humans to fully understand or

dismantle. As a result, daily life reorganizes

around these electricity access periods.

**Activities such as lighting, cooking,**

**communication, and work begin to follow the**

**rhythm of electricity availability rather than the**

**traditional idea of constant supply**.

Electricity infrastructure today is already

becoming increasingly automated and

dependent on algorithmic management.

Smart grids, **predictive energy systems**,

and automated distribution networks are

being developed to balance supply and

demand more efficiently. At the same

time, rising global energy consumption

and climate-related disruptions are

making energy production less stable in

many parts of the world.

**Because electricity cannot easily be**

**stored at extremely large scales, power**

**systems must constantly balance**

**production and consumption**. This

speculation extends these current

trends into a future where electricity

becomes a carefully managed planetary

resource, distributed through automated

systems that coordinate energy

availability across different regions.

**This project encourages readers to**

**notice how dependent modern societies**

**already are on complex infrastructure**

**systems that most people rarely see or**

**think about. Electricity appears constant**

**and effortless in everyday life, but it**

**depends on vast networks of power**

**plants, transmission systems, and**

**automated technologies that operate**

**beyond the control of ordinary citizens.**

By imagining a world where electricity

arrives only during specific access

windows, the project highlights how

fragile and invisible these infrastructures

actually are. by

automated systems. **It also asks readers to**

**reflect on how communities might adapt**

**their habits and social structures if**

**access to basic resources becomes**

**limited, scheduled, or governed**
