# **1111 Maybe**

**Untitled.Bhakti**

Why should land remain idle when food

demand never stops?

In a world driven by continuous consumption and global supply chains, agricultural production is tightly aligned with industrial food systems.

**The Seasonal Labour Allocation System** links farmers and agricultural workers directly to large-scale harvesting cycles. During harvest periods, workers labor intensively to meet the demands of global food production and distribution.

**However, once the harvest ends and the land rests, and the workers are put into bio pods and sent into status.** 

During this time, they remain registered within the labour system but essentially frozen \- so as not to age or become decrepit and lose their value in fallow times. Food production continues at global scale, but the people producing it experience cycles of intense labour followed by long periods of being frozen. *This of course has the added advantage of never having to account for the food that those doing the labour of harvesting might consume when  there is nothing to harvest.*

In this world, labour and rest are no longer natural or personal. They are measured, distributed, and controlled. 

Farming has always been seasonal, with intense labour during planting and harvesting followed by periods with little or no work. As food production becomes more industrialized, crops are increasingly grown through large supply chains and contracts that require synchronized harvesting within short time frames.

Many agricultural workers today are employed through temporary or seasonal labour systems, where work exists only during certain production periods. As food systems scale for efficiency, labour becomes organized around harvest cycles rather than stable employment, leaving workers waiting between seasons.

**Large-scale food systems appear continuous in markets, but the labour behind them is often unstable and seasonal.** Farmers and agricultural workers face uncertain income and depend heavily on harvest cycles, while industrial food production prioritizes efficiency and scale over their working conditions.

This speculation exaggerates that reality to ask a troubling question: **what happens when the people responsible for feeding society are themselves trapped in cycles of waiting between the seasons that sustain the global food system?**
